# Reflectance Spectra Database for Pigments Checker “Modern & Contemporary Art”

#### Scientific Art Examination with Imaging and Spectroscopy
Published Jul 4, 2022
[+ Follow](https://www.linkedin.com/signup/cold-join?session_redirect=/pulse/reflectance-spectra-database-pigments-checker-modern-art-cosentino&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_publisher-author-card)
## CHSOS Application note #4
Just published on our website the Reflectance Spectra Database for the new[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)“Modern & Contemporary Art”. This note discusses some of these spectra and highlights the most important findings.
Used from the late 80′ [1] Reflectance Spectroscopy ([RS](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/reflectance-spectroscopy-system/&urlhash=7BWn&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)) is a powerful tool for the identification of pigments and dyes [2]. A reflectance spectrum shows for each wavelength the ratio between the intensity of the reflected and incident radiation. This ratio is called reflectance and is given in percentage (%). Pigments are identified using a spectral database and comparing the spectral features of the investigated unknown spectrum with the spectra available in the database.
The advantages of this method with respect to the other spectroscopic methods are: affordable equipment, small dimension, and portability.
## [DOWNLOAD: Application note #4: Gorgias Reflectance Spectra Database for Pigments Checker Modern & Contemporary Art](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/download/29943/&urlhash=va0b&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)“Modern & Contemporary Art”
This is a collection of the most important pigments used in modern & contemporary art. On the other hand, the STANDARD[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is a collection of the most used pigments from prehistory to contemporary art, and consequently, it has just a few modern pigments. This new checker is focused solely on modern pigments and completes those already included in the standard[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). The colors are laid with an acrylic binder on a cardboard support. We collected the spectra of the pigments and that of the binder alone on the cardboard. All these spectra are available online on the[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)webpage.
## Pigments with noticeable characterizing features
Figure [1] shows the[Gorgias](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/reflectance-spectroscopy-system/&urlhash=7BWn&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)system acquiring spectra on the new[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).
Some pigments feature unique and characterizing features. The spectra of 4 modern pigments are reported in Figure [2].
PBr 24 –chrome titanate, available from 1946, has a strong and large absorption band centered at 730 nm.
PG 36 –[phthalo green](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/phthalo-green-k-23000/&urlhash=HI7H&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)YS, has great lightfastness and it is always used with extenders and fillers to make it more workable. It is a bromated and chlorinated copper phthalocyanine that features a characterizing maximum close to the UV region at 410 nm.
PY 53 –nickel-titanium yellow, is a nickel antimony titanium rutile pigment, used as a replacement for the historical[naples yellow](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/naples-yellow-k-10130/&urlhash=150W&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). It features a large and strong absorption band centered at 740 nm.
PY 150 –nickel azo yellow, a monoazo nickel complex, is used as a replacement for the original Indian yellow since it has the same brownish tint. It is identified by its unique 3 maxima at 600 nm, 650 nm, and 715 nm.

## [Titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), rutile, or anatase?
[Titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is a general name to indicate a white pigment that is made of one (or a mixture) of the two forms of titanium oxide: anatase and rutile. These crystals have the same chemical formula, TiO2, but different relative positioning of the 3 atoms (they are 2 polymorphs of titanium oxide).
The standard[Pigments Checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)v.5 has the rutile[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). Nowadays[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is all made from rutile. But this was not always the case. From about 1920[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)was made mostly of anatase because it was easier to produce. From about 1940 a cheap production method for rutile was established and it replaced anatase since it was more lightfast and had a stronger hiding power.
The new “modern and contemporary art”[pigments checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)has the “anatase”[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).
Figure [3] shows the reflectance spectra of the two types of[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), anatase, and rutile acquired on the two pigments checkers, “standard” and “modern & contemporary art”. The two types of[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)can be distinguished by their reflectance spectra since the absorption band in the UV has the inflection point at 405 nm for rutile and 380 nm for anatase.
We did test the[Gorgias](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/reflectance-spectroscopy-system/&urlhash=7BWn&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)spectrometer on an Indian Mughal manuscript which was supposed to be from the 18th century, likely 1790, figure [4].
We were interested in evaluating the authenticity of the item and, in particular, its dating.

As shown in figure [3] the spectrum of the white paint (point 4) is comparable to the spectrum of anatase. This type of[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)was also confirmed with the Raman Spectroscopy system[ElviRA](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/raman-spectrometer&urlhash=x9R2&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).
We know that anatase was in use from roughly 1920’-1940’. For a manuscript supposedly made in the 18th century, we were expecting[lead white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/lead-white-k-46000/&urlhash=zr7z&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)pigment, definitely not anatase. Furthermore, the identification of anatase indicates that the object was created not much later than the 1940’, otherwise, we should have found more of the rutile[titanium white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/titanium-white-k-46200/&urlhash=qOIs&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block).

## Red and yellow pigments
The red pigments, figure [5], feature the usual spectrum for the red color. They absorb the blue and the green spectrum and reflect light starting from the yellow region, from about 600 nm. Though, they can be distinguished by the position of their respective inflection points.
The same can be said for the yellow pigments, as shown in figure [6].

## White Pigments
The new[pigments checker](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/pigments-checker/&urlhash=pfXy&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)has 4 whites. We discussed already PW 6 –anatase. PW 7-[zinc white](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/zinc-white-k-46300/&urlhash=Mtul&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is remarkable for the strong absorbance bands in the red-infrared region 650 –750 nm. This material is not used alone as a paint but it is found with other pigments to provide them with hiding power, such as for[lithopone](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/lithopone-k-46100/&urlhash=fVtf&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). This last one is a mixture of barium sulfate and zinc sulfide. PW 11- antimony white, used as an artist pigment from 1920, its characterized by its flat curve that makes it a very “real” white. Figure [8] illustrates the case of[lithopone](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/lithopone-k-46100/&urlhash=fVtf&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block), which we just introduced at the beginning of this paragraph while discussing the peculiar absorption bands of the zinc sulfide.
[Lithopone](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/lithopone-k-46100/&urlhash=fVtf&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is a mixture of barium sulfate which has a pretty featureless spectrum and zinc sulfide. The resulting spectrum of[lithopone](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/lithopone-k-46100/&urlhash=fVtf&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)shows the zinc sulfide features since barium sulfate is pretty flat.

## Conclusions
[Gorgias](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/reflectance-spectroscopy-system/&urlhash=7BWn&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)is a portable and low-cost reflectance spectroscopy system. We suggest using[Gorgias](https://www.linkedin.com/redir/redirect?url=https://chsopensource.org/reflectance-spectroscopy-system/&urlhash=7BWn&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)for most applications and in particular for those that require mobility and travel with the equipment. The analysis of the new set of modern pigments confirms that even with these modern paints, reflectance spectroscopy is a valid tool for, at least, a preliminary characterization of the colors.
